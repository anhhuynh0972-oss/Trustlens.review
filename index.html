The problem

More information. But can we trust it?

TikTok Shop combines creator content, product recommendations and customer reviews. However, Gen Z consumers may struggle to distinguish paid promotions from genuine reviews and assess product quality before purchasing.

Real review or paid promotion?

Creator content, ads and customer reviews sit side by side, so genuine experiences are hard to tell apart from promotion.

Good quality or just a trend?

A product can be popular without being good. Its real quality is uncertain until it arrives.

Repeated or biased reviews

Very similar wording, waves of short five-star reviews or little negative feedback make it hard to judge how balanced the reviews are.

Which seller is reliable?

Seller information is scattered and partly self-reported, so credibility is hard to judge before buying.

Information overload

Videos, livestreams, ratings and hundreds of reviews: too much to read before a quick purchase.

Target users: aged 18–24 · university students · active TikTok users · regular online shoppers

Academic evidence

A meta-analysis of 156 studies (214 effect sizes, 69,006 observations) found that every review factor studied affects purchase intention, and that review valence (how positive or negative reviews are) has the strongest effect.

3.32

In a survey of 361 Vietnamese students in 10 Mekong Delta provinces and cities (January–March 2024), “Shopping on TikTok Shop can be trusted” had the lowest mean of all 28 items. Trust correlated with online impulse buying (r = .524); this is a correlation, not proof of cause.

Limitations

  • The industry reports are commercial online surveys; their samples and methods are only partly published.
  • Cimigo's sample covers ages 18–44, wider than TrustLens's 18–24 target group.
  • Qiu & Zhang (2024) is a global meta-analysis, not specific to Vietnam; we cite only its abstract.
  • Phan et al. (2024) is a regional convenience sample, and its trust–impulse link is correlational.
  • None of these sources measures whether TrustLens itself improves shopping decisions.

Technology stack

Review retrieval

Accessible reviews are fetched on the server through a licensed data provider, page by page, with no scraping.

AI & NLP

Google Gemini extracts sentiment, themes and recurring complaints, in batches that cover every retrieved review.

Data analytics

Counts, rating distributions and review patterns are computed from the actual records, never estimated.

Transparency signals

Each finding links to original review excerpts and IDs, and flags conflicting feedback with its evidence.

Our proposed solution

TrustLens: an AI-powered product review & credibility assistant

TrustLens is a proposed AI-powered decision-support tool that helps Vietnamese Gen Z evaluate product and seller credibility before purchasing on TikTok Shop.

Core features

AI Review Summary

Summarises recurring customer experiences and complaints, and says how many reviews were analysed.

Needs: review text (retrieved or pasted) and AI configured on the server.

Seller Credibility Indicators

Organises available seller information and review patterns into transparent signals. No invented trust score.

Needs: seller fields from the data source or entered by you; otherwise shown as “not available”.

Potential Red-Flag Alerts

Highlights conflicting reviews or recurring concerns for further investigation.

Needs: several reviews showing the same pattern.

A red flag is a potential indicator, not proof of fraud.

Key Information Highlights

Links generated insights to supporting reviews where available, with original excerpts and review IDs.

Needs: the original review text and IDs; AI wording is always labelled as AI.

FAQ

Questions about TrustLens

What this prototype does today, and where its limits are.

How does TrustLens work?+

You paste a product's reviews or open a demo product. TrustLens sorts each review by sentiment, groups recurring topics, runs a set of red-flag checks and builds a report that links every finding to the reviews behind it. For a product link, TrustLens retrieves the reviews through a licensed data provider, counts them, and sends them in batches to Google Gemini on the server for an evidence-linked summary. Reviews you paste or import go through the same AI steps. Demo products use only the rule-based checks in your browser and are never sent anywhere.

Can TrustLens analyse every TikTok Shop product?+

Only products the data provider can access. Coverage, page limits and credits depend on the provider, and TrustLens shows exactly how many reviews it retrieved versus how many the source reports. If a link fails, you can paste or import reviews instead. It reads Vietnamese and English, recognises most Vietnamese keywords with or without diacritics, and gives steadier results with 10 or more reviews. Slang, sarcasm, emoji-only reviews and other languages may be misread.

How does TrustLens identify potential suspicious reviews?+

It looks for near-identical wording across different reviews, a high share of very short generic praise, star ratings that contradict the review text, mentions of vouchers or gifts given for reviews, several reviews saying the product differs from its listing, and claims that the product is fake. Each flag lists the reviews that triggered it.

Does a red flag prove a review is fake?+

No. A red flag is a potential indicator, not proof. Real buyers often use the same short phrases, and a listing can be accurate even when a few people disagree with it. Treat a flag as a reason to read more closely, look at buyer photos and videos, and compare other sellers.

What happens if review data cannot be retrieved?+

TrustLens says that no data was retrieved and asks you to paste the reviews instead. It never fills the gap with made-up reviews, ratings or seller details. If retrieval stops part-way (for example at a page limit or when provider credits run out), TrustLens keeps the reviews it did retrieve, marks the result as partial and says why.

How does TrustLens handle user data?+

Demo products are analysed in your browser. Product links and reviews you paste or import are sent to our server, which retrieves reviews through the data provider and analyses them with Google Gemini; API keys stay on the server. If you save a report, it's stored in this browser's local storage until you delete it. TrustLens doesn't ask for your name, account or TikTok login.
